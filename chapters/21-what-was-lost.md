# What Was Lost

*Chapter Twenty-One — The Fane That Waited*

---

The evening was the only hour Drezen was quiet, and Nageru had learned to keep it.

By day the fortress was a hammer on a hundred anvils. Masons on the walls. Carters in the gates. Sergeants in every courtyard with lists. By night it was a watch-fire and a patrol and the long wind off the Wound. But between the two there was an hour when the work set itself down and the people set themselves down beside it, and the stone gave back the heat of the day, and nobody wanted anything from anyone.

He sat on the third step of the east courtyard stair with his hands open on his knees.

It was not meditation. His masters would not have called it that. It was only sitting, and breathing, and letting the courtyard be what it was. A soldier mending a boot by lantern. Two paladins arguing about a horse. Korroc across the flagstones on an upturned crate by the well, a mug in one hand, laughing at something a teamster had said, his laugh going up off the stone and into the violet sky.

Nageru breathed in.

And the courtyard was gone.

---

A cave. Red light. Heat pressing on his face like a hand.

He knew it before he knew he knew it. The chasm under the Weeping Hills. The ledge. The fifty-foot pit. The slab of smoky crystal where a dead priestess lay with her hands crossed on her breast and a bow laid along her side.

It stood before him. Close enough to touch.

And it was melting.

Not cracking. Not breaking. *Melting* — the smoke in it thinning, the edges running like wax at the lip of a candle, the whole shape of it slumping and sliding and going soft, so that the woman inside was no longer inside anything at all —

Gone.

The courtyard. The step. The lantern and the boot and the argument about the horse.

His hands were still open on his knees. His breath was still in his chest, the same breath, not yet let out. No time had passed. He was quite sure of that. The paladins had not finished their sentence.

He let the breath go, slowly, all of it.

Across the courtyard, Korroc had stopped laughing.

The dwarf sat on his crate with the mug halfway to his mouth and his eyes on nothing, and the teamster was still talking at him and he was not hearing a word. Then Korroc blinked, and set the mug down on the flagstones very carefully, as a man sets down a thing he has just discovered he might drop.

He looked up across the courtyard.

Their eyes met.

Neither of them said anything. Nageru did not get up, and Korroc did not come over, and after a moment the dwarf picked his mug back up and the teamster laughed at his own joke and the evening went on.

Nageru sat with his hands on his knees until the stars came out, and did not reach for an explanation.

*Expectation is a poor substitute for observation.* Sister Lyra had written that to him, in her plain upright hand, not so very long ago. He had not known then how often he would have to use it.

He had observed a thing.

He did not yet know what it was.

That would have to be enough for one night.

---

In the morning there was a map.

Aron Kir had it pinned flat on the command table with four daggers, which was the kind of thing Aron did, and Anevia Tirabade stood over it with her arms folded and the look of a woman who had not slept much and did not intend to discuss it.

"Long-range scouts came in at dawn," she said. No greeting. Anevia did not waste the morning on greetings. "Thirty miles west of here, there appears to be an active temple of Baphomet."

She put one finger on the map. Nageru looked where she pointed and saw nothing, a scatter of hatching and a line that meant a drop.

"Two days out. Maybe a bit more with a wagon." Anevia looked up at the four of them in turn. "It could give us something worth having. Names. Orders. Where things are coming from. But whether it does or not, clearing out an active temple that close to us isn't optional. It's full of cultists, and it's a staging ground waiting to happen."

Nobody spoke for a moment.

Caleth was looking at the map. Nageru watched him read it — the half-elf's eyes went over it the way they went over everything, measuring, counting, and then the counting finished and his face settled.

"We can do that," Caleth said, and nodded. "There's no reason to wait. Let's get ready and go."

Thane made a small noise that might have been agreement.

Korroc said, "Aye."

Nageru inclined his head.

He had a letter folded in the wrappings at his waist. He had carried it every day since it came. It asked him to find a small house of his god somewhere in the lands near Drezen, and it gave him no road to find it by.

He did not think of the letter. He was careful not to.

*A temple of Baphomet, thirty miles west.*

That was all it was.

---

The wagon was waiting within the hour, with two horses in the traces and Caleth's riding horse tied on behind, and they went out through the west gate into the Worldwound without anyone watching them go.

The first half of the day was easy, as days out here went. The ground was hard and the sky was the sick bruised color it always was and nothing came at them. Thane drove. Korroc sat up beside him and complained about the seat. Caleth rode alongside. Nageru walked, because walking was the thing his body understood, and the wagon was not fast.

About ten miles out, the road bent past a heap of rock.

It was more than a heap. It was the size of a small hill, a steep broken knuckle of stone pushed up out of the plain, its sides sheer and cut with ledges, and the wind went around it in a low moan.

And then the moan was not the wind.

Screaming.

Nageru stopped walking.

It came from everywhere and nowhere, thin and far off, and for a moment it seemed to be riding on the wind from some great distance. Then the next scream came and he heard it strike the rock face to his left and come back off it, and he understood that it was not far away at all. It was bouncing. It was close.

It was above them.

*Roars*, under the screams. Rage. Something enormous and furious.

And over both — carrying, ringing, unmistakable — a stream of *Dwarven*.

Nageru did not speak Dwarven. But he had spent a year beside Korroc and Thane, and he did not need to speak it to know what it sounded like when it was being used for the purpose most dwarves seemed to believe it had been invented for. This was that. Someone up on the top of that rock was cursing at a volume and a length and an inventiveness he had never heard even from Korroc, who was not a small man in that regard.

Korroc was on his feet on the wagon box, staring upward, his mouth open.

"That's —" he said. "Who in the *Father's* name —"

Caleth cupped a hand to his mouth.

"Is everyone all right up there?"

The Dwarven stopped for a heartbeat.

A claw came over the ledge.

It was huge. It was the shape of a lobster's pincer, if a lobster had been grown in a furnace and taught to hate, and it closed on the lip of the rock with a crunch Nageru felt in his teeth. Then a head came up after it: long, scaled, crowned with horns that swept back like a ram's, and a mouth that opened to show him far too many teeth.

It looked down at them.

It roared.

"Glabrezu," Caleth said, very quietly.

The head vanished back behind the rock.

For one breath nothing happened.

Then the sky went dark over the wagon, and it was *overhead* — it had come off the top of that rock like a stone out of a sling, huge, four arms and a tail and a pair of claws spread wide, and it went over them close enough that the wind of its passage lifted Nageru's hair, and it came down in the dirt just past the wagon with a sound like a building falling over.

The horses screamed.

And from the top of the rock, undaunted, the Dwarven started up again.

---

Thane's voice rose behind him in a short hard prayer, and something warm and bright went over the four of them like sunlight through water.

Nageru was already moving.

He did not decide to. There was a thing in front of him that had come to kill, and his body understood that, and his body went. He crossed the ground between them in three long strides and a leap, and the glabrezu's long arm came around to meet him — it was *fast*, it was so much faster than something that size should be — and the smaller clawed hand at the end of it caught him across the ribs as he came in.

He twisted. The claws raked him instead of opening him. He felt them go along his side like hot wire.

And then he was inside its reach, where the long arms could not fold back far enough, and he brought his fist down on it.

The blow went in. He felt it go.

The thing did not seem to notice.

Korroc arrived beside him with a clatter and a grunt, legs pumping, shield up — and the glabrezu swung at the dwarf almost idly, and the blow rang off the shield and went nowhere.

Then it stopped being idle.

Nageru saw it gather. He saw it as he saw every strike, a half-second before it came: the weight settling, the shoulders turning, the breath going in. And there was nothing he could do with the half-second, because what came was not one strike.

Two great pincers. Two clawed hands. A mouth.

All at once.

They came split between him and Korroc and every one of them landed. A pincer closed on Nageru's shoulder and let go. A hand opened his thigh. The teeth came down on Korroc's shield arm and found the seam of the armor and the dwarf roared. It was like standing under a rockfall. It was like being beaten by five men who had trained together all their lives.

Nageru stood.

Blood was coming down his side. He breathed around it.

In the corner of his eye he saw Caleth off the horse and running, low, using the thing's attention on the two of them to close the distance, coming in behind Korroc's shoulder with Radiance already up. And past Caleth, wide, going out and around in a long curve the way he always did, a smaller shape that was Thane.

*Good*, Nageru thought.

Then he stopped thinking.

---

He reached down into the place where his order had taught him to reach, below the breath and below the blood, and the fire answered.

It came up his arms. It came as it always came, not as heat but as *light with weight in it*, running out along the bones of his forearms and pooling in his fists until his hands were two white coals, and the glabrezu's slit eyes went to them and widened.

He struck three times.

Right. Left. Right. Holy fire and a monk's whole body behind each blow, and each one landed, and each one went into the demon like a brand into wet hide, and the smell of it filled the air.

The glabrezu screamed.

Korroc's hammer came around and slammed into its flank — a blow that would have dropped a horse — and the demon did not even turn its head.

Its eyes were on Nageru.

He felt the moment it happened. He felt its attention settle on him the way a stone settles on a man's chest, total and deliberate. It had looked at the four of them and it had made a choice, and the choice was him. *That one. That one first.*

*Very well*, Nageru thought.

Both pincers came.

They closed on him high and low and he could not get out from between them, and they bit, and the world went white at the edges. He heard something that might have been his own breath going out of him.

And then the ground let go.

---

It was not the ground. It was *down*.

Down stopped being where it had been.

Nageru's feet stayed where they were, because he was in the demon's grip. But beyond the demon, Korroc and Caleth and Thane simply *fell*. They fell up. They went off the ground like dust off a struck rug, arms out, weapons flailing, and they fell and fell into the bruised sky, and Korroc's bellow went up with them and got smaller and smaller —

A hundred feet. More.

Nageru, pinned in the claws, watched his three friends tumble helplessly away overhead, a hundred and forty feet up, three small shapes turning over against the clouds.

And one of those shapes reached out, and caught hold of the other two, and folded.

The air cracked.

They were on the ground. All three of them. Behind the glabrezu, where they had not been a heartbeat ago — Caleth on one knee with a fistful of Korroc's collar in one hand and Thane's belt in the other, looking as if he might be sick, and both dwarves staggering, and Thane already turning, already moving, already sliding in low under the tail.

Two knives went into the demon's back.

It roared and jerked its head around to look — just once, just for an instant, to see what had stung it —

— and Nageru, who had been waiting for exactly that, reached for the fire again and brought it down on the side of its skull.

The glabrezu *screamed*. Not rage now. Frustration. A creature that could not understand why the thing in front of it would not simply die.

It let go of him.

He did not fall. He was quite proud of that, later, in the privacy of his own thoughts. His legs held.

But he was hurt, and he knew it. There was a great deal of blood.

On the far side of the demon, Korroc had gone still.

Nageru felt it. He felt the thread that ran between him and the dwarf — the working Korroc laid on all of them, the one that carried a share of each wound home to him — go taut, and then pull, as Korroc felt the state of him down the line and *reached*. A wave of warmth broke over Nageru from nowhere, gold and thick as honey, and some of the torn places closed.

Some.

Korroc was moving, coming around the demon's hindquarters with his shield up and his face set, taking what he could through the thread and coming for more. But the glabrezu was vast, and he was on the wrong side of it, and his legs were short.

He was not going to get there.

Nageru saw the dwarf see that.

And then the glabrezu struck him again, very hard, and Nageru's vision went gray, and he felt his blood leaving him not in drops now but in a flow, warm down his side and his leg and into the dirt.

He stayed on his feet.

He was not sure how.

---

Caleth came in from the side.

Nageru saw it through the gray: the half-elf, still pale from the fold, with no glaive raised and nothing in his hands at all, walking in under the demon's arm the way a man walks up to a door. His face was very calm. His face was the face Nageru had seen on him once or twice before, when Caleth had stopped counting because the count had come out, and all that remained was to do the arithmetic's work.

Caleth put out his hand and laid it flat on the glabrezu's side.

There was a sound like the sky tearing.

Light — blue-white, crawling — came out of Caleth's palm and went *into* the thing, and ran, and spread, and every scale on its body stood up at once. It went rigid. Its four arms flung out. Its jaws locked open on a scream that did not come.

And then it was gone.

Not fallen. Gone. There was a shape in the air that was a demon and then there was a shape in the air that was smoke, and then there was not even that, only a hot bad smell and a dark scorched patch on the ground and Caleth standing in front of it with his hand still out, breathing hard.

Nageru sat down.

It was not a decision.

---

The Dwarven had not stopped.

Through the whole of it — through the charge and the claws and the falling sky — it had gone on up there, a furious unbroken stream, and now in the sudden quiet it was the only sound for a mile.

Korroc cupped his hands and shouted something up at the rock in the same language. Nageru did not understand a word of it, but he knew the shape of a question.

The cursing went on as though no one had spoken.

"The demon is dead!" Caleth called up. "It's gone! You can come down!"

The cursing went on.

Thane looked at the rock. He looked at Korroc. He shrugged — one small economical shrug that said, as clearly as words, *well, somebody has to* — and put his hand to his belt where his silver scale hung, and closed his fingers on it.

And rose.

Terendelev's gift took him up off the ground like a feather in a chimney draft. He went up slowly, straight up, and as he went he kept one hand on the rock face and pulled himself in close to it, so he would not drift, the way a swimmer keeps a hand on the side of a pool. Nageru watched him go up past the first ledge, and the second, and over the top.

The cursing stopped.

For a long moment there was no sound at all.

Then — faint, carrying, from the top of the rock — a voice that was not Thane's, and was not one voice.

It sounded like several dwarves talking at once. It sounded *delighted*.

Korroc turned to Nageru with an expression of absolute bafflement.

"What," the dwarf said, "is he *doing* up there?"

Nageru had no answer.

When Thane came back over the edge he was carrying something, and he floated down the rock face with it held out a little from his body, turning it over in his hands as he came. His feet touched the ground.

It was an axe.

A dwarven battleaxe, heavy-headed, the haft bound in old leather. And it was *talking*. Muttering. A low grumble of many voices that rose and fell and overlapped, like a hall full of old dwarves arguing about something they had been arguing about for three hundred years. They seemed calmer now. Almost content.

Thane's face was doing something Nageru had not seen it do before.

It was not a large thing. Thane's face never did large things. But there was an ease in it — a loosening around the eyes — as if the axe in his hands had set down a weight Thane had not known he was carrying.

"Fiendsplitter," Thane said.

He said it as a man says the name of an old friend. As though no one had needed to tell him.

"There was a wagon up there," he went on, more like himself. "Or there was. The demon dragged it up and smashed it. Bits of armor. Torn open." He paused. "And this, lying in the middle of it, shouting at me. First thing it said was *come here, I want to drink that demon's blood*." He looked down at the axe. "Bit late for that."

The axe grumbled.

Thane turned it so the flat of the head caught the light, and held it out toward his cousin.

"It's got Torag's hammer and anvil on it," he said. "Embossed. Right there. You could use it for your holy symbol."

Korroc looked at the axe.

Then he reached over without a word and knocked twice on his own shield, where the Father's hammer and anvil stood out in the metal, and shrugged.

Thane snorted. He swung the axe up and over and strapped it across his back, and the many voices muttered to themselves about that, and then fell quiet.

Korroc was already turning away from the axe and toward Nageru, pulling a wand from his belt as he came.

He touched it to his own chest first, briefly, and gold light ran down into the torn place at his shield arm and closed it. Then he crouched in front of Nageru where he sat in the dirt, and put the wand against the long wet ruin of his side, and spoke the word.

Warmth went into him. It went in deep, all the way down, gold and heavy, and Nageru felt the claw-marks along his ribs draw closed and the opened thigh knit and the torn shoulder settle back into itself. Korroc did it again. And a third time, grunting, until he was satisfied, the way a smith goes back over a weld he does not quite trust.

"There," Korroc said. "That's better. It didn't like you."

"No," Nageru agreed.

"Can't think why."

Nageru got up.

---

They made camp as the light went, in the lee of a long low ridge, with the wagon drawn across the open side and the horses picketed close.

They had just sat down to eat.

Korroc stood up.

He stood so suddenly that his bowl tipped off his knee and into the dirt, and he did not look at it. He was looking up, past the fire, at the moon — the thin bone-colored moon low over the Wound — and he flung out an arm and pointed.

"*See?*" Korroc said. "*That's* what I saw!"

They looked.

Something crossed the moon.

It was big. It was very big. It was a shape with wings, far away and very high, and for the space of two breaths its outline stood black against the pale disc of the moon, and then it was past, and the moon was clean, and the sky was only sky.

Nobody said anything.

Nageru looked at Caleth. Caleth was looking at Thane. Thane was looking at Korroc. Korroc was still pointing at an empty moon.

Then the dwarf lowered his arm, and sat back down, and looked at the bowl in the dirt, and sighed.

The four of them settled back in around the fire.

Nobody said what it was. Nobody knew. Nageru thought that each of them was probably thinking of a list of things it might have been, and that none of the lists were short, and none of them were comfortable.

After the food, Caleth got up and went around the fire to Korroc and held out his hand.

Korroc looked at the hand, and then at Caleth, and then sighed and dug the healing wand out of his belt and gave it to him.

Caleth sat down beside the dwarf with the wand across his palms. He closed his fingers around it. He did not speak and he did not move, and for a long moment nothing at all seemed to be happening, except that his face went very still and very inward, the way it went when he was listening for something a long way off.

Then Nageru felt it. A small warmth, a small pressure in the air, like the moment before a candle takes.

Caleth opened his hand and gave the wand back.

Korroc turned it over. He weighed it in his palm the way he weighed a hammer, and his eyebrows went up.

"Aye," he said. "There's charge back in that."

Caleth only nodded, and rubbed his eyes.

Nobody asked how. Nobody ever did. Nageru had watched Aravashnial ask for twenty minutes on a forge floor and get *I don't know* for his trouble.

He took the second watch.

The moon went down without anything else crossing it.

---

In the morning, as they broke camp, Caleth came over to where Thane was checking the traces and held out a folded paper.

Thane looked at it. Then at him.

"The map," Caleth said.

Thane took it. "Did you look at it?"

Caleth shook his head. "That's your job." And he grinned — a real grin, quick and rather boyish, the kind he did not spend often — and walked away to his horse.

Thane rolled his eyes so hard Nageru nearly heard it. He unfolded the map.

He stood for a minute with the paper spread in both hands. He looked at it, and then up at the land around them, and then at the rising sun low and red over their right shoulders, and then back at the paper, and his lips moved very slightly as if he were counting.

Then he folded it once and pointed.

"That way."

Korroc, already up on the wagon box, looked down at him.

"Are you sure?"

Thane did not answer. He simply looked at his cousin — a long, flat, patient look — and climbed up onto the wagon beside him, and took the reins, and went that way.

---

Early on the third day, the ground ahead of them simply stopped.

Thane saw it first. He hauled the horses in and stood up on the box.

"It's just a little ahead," he called back.

He jumped down. Nageru walked up to join him at the front of the wagon, and a moment later Caleth swung down off his horse and came up on Thane's other side, and the three of them stood looking forward at a line in the land where the plain ended and the air began.

A chasm. Or a long cliff falling away. From here there was only the edge, and the far side of nothing.

Korroc came down off the wagon with the unhurried heaviness of a dwarf who knew he was about to be asked for something.

Thane turned to him. He pointed at a big split boulder off to one side of the road.

"Cousin," he said. "Put the wagon and horses behind that?"

Korroc looked at him.

"Am I your stable boy now?"

Thane snorted. "No. But you're loud as hell." He considered. "Even *in* hell."

Korroc gave him a long, flat, silent look, which Nageru recognized as the same look Thane had given Korroc the morning before, returned with interest.

Then the dwarf took the horses by their reins and led them off toward the boulder, muttering in Dwarven, and his armor creaked with every step, and Thane watched him go with the expression of a man whose point had been made for him.

---

The three of them went to the edge on foot.

They went slowly and they went quietly. Thane went low, almost on his knees, and Caleth went carefully behind him, and Nageru went as he always went, which was without sound, because silence was a thing his feet had been taught before they had been taught to walk.

At the edge, the land fell away into a wide steep valley, a great bowl gouged out of the plain, its far wall a sheer face of dark stone. A path went down the near side — a real path, cut and worn, switching back and forth across the slope.

And at the bottom, against the far wall, in plain sight, was the temple.

It was built into the cliff itself. A dark mouth in the rock, very wide, with steps going up to it and figures standing on either side of the steps — statues — and no movement anywhere. No smoke. No sentries. Nothing.

It was early. The valley floor was still in shadow.

Behind them, Korroc's armor creaked.

The dwarf was coming across the ground toward them, having hidden the wagon, and every step he took was a small metallic complaint. Thane turned his head and looked at him. So did Caleth. Neither said anything.

Then all three of them started down the path, and as they went, Thane put his hand out behind him, low, palm back, and patted the air twice. *Stay back. Give us room.*

Korroc, to his very great credit, stayed back.

---

They were halfway down when Nageru saw the two men.

They were below and ahead, on a lower switchback of the same path, walking down it toward the temple. Two cultists. Dark robes.

Nageru stopped. Everyone stopped. Thane went flat to the path. Caleth went still against the rock. Nageru simply ceased moving, which was a thing he could do in a way that made most eyes slide off him.

The two men did not look up.

They did not look around. They walked down the rest of the path at an ordinary pace, talking in low voices, and crossed the valley floor, and went up the steps between the statues, and into the dark mouth of the temple.

They did not look back.

Nageru let out the breath he had been holding.

They went on down.

---

At the foot of the path the temple stood before them: a short flight of stairs, flanked on either side by a tall carved statue worn nearly featureless by wind, climbing up to an opening in the northern cliff face some thirty feet wide.

They crept to the foot of the stairs.

Caleth stopped there. He closed his eyes for a moment and Nageru watched something gather in him — not a spell, not yet; a readying, a drawing-in, the way a man fills his lungs before going under water.

Thane went on up the stairs alone.

He went up them the way Thane went up anything, low and to one side and in the shadow of the right-hand statue, and at the top he put his back to the stone beside the great opening and eased his head around it, one eye, for the space of a heartbeat.

He drew back.

He turned to them, and held up one hand, fingers spread.

Then the other hand, one finger.

*Six.*

Caleth opened his eyes. He reached out, put one hand on Korroc's shoulder — the dwarf had come up quietly behind them after all — and the other on Nageru's arm.

And the world folded.

---

Nageru came out of the fold standing beside Thane, inside the temple, and grief hit him like a wave hitting a wall.

It came out of nowhere. It came out of the *stone*. It came up through the soles of his bare feet and went into him and filled him — a sorrow so old and so deep that for one breath he could not see, and under the sorrow, hot and red, a rage. Not his rage. He had never been here. He did not know this place. And the place was grieving at him, and the place was *furious*, and he did not understand why.

He breathed.

He made himself see.

A great chamber of worked stone, the ceiling going up into shadow thirty feet over his head. Torches in iron brackets on the walls, burning a sullen flickering red. And against the northern wall, filling it floor to ceiling, a statue.

A goat-headed demon, seated on a stone platform seven feet high, flanked by smaller statues on either side. Horned. Leering. Carved with a craftsman's care.

Seated with its legs crossed, each foot upon the opposite thigh.

Its hands resting open on its knees.

Nageru saw that, and something in his chest went cold and still, and he did not yet know why that either.

Two men had turned around.

Their eyes went wide. Their mouths opened. They shouted.

And the temple woke up.

---

They came with glaives.

Two of them, first, running, bringing the long blades down and forward, and one came at Korroc and one came at Nageru. Nageru twisted. The glaive went past his hip and found nothing. The other one went in over the rim of Korroc's shield and scored along his arm, and Korroc swore.

Thane was already gone — tumbling past the first man's legs, coming up behind him with the knives, and the man jerked and shouted.

Nageru put his fists into the one in front of him.

The man folded around them. He went down on the flagstones, and stayed there.

Korroc's hammer went across the other man twice — *crack, crack* — and that one reeled.

Then there were more.

They came out of doorways in the side walls. They came from behind the pillars. Robes and armor and blades and the bull's head of Baphomet on their chests. A sword came at Nageru and he did not see it soon enough, and it opened his arm. Another man came at Korroc and his blow rang off the shield. A third came in behind Thane with a glaive, and Thane, without apparently looking, twisted aside from both his thrusts.

A fourth came at Korroc from the other side.

That made three on the dwarf.

Korroc could not bring his shield around to all of them. Nageru saw it happen: the glaive coming in low from the left where the shield was not, and finding the seam at Korroc's side where the plates met, and driving — a killing thrust, a blow meant to go in to the spine —

And stopping.

The point went in. It broke the skin. And it went no further, as though it had struck stone under the flesh. Which it had. Nageru had seen it before and it still made the hair stand up on his arms. The templar stared at his own glaive stuck an inch into a dwarf and could not believe it.

Radiance came over Korroc's shoulder like a shaft of sun.

Caleth, behind the dwarf, thrust twice over him — past his helm, past his ear — and both thrusts went into the second of Korroc's attackers, hard, and the man shrieked.

Another templar came out of a side room behind Thane and put a glaive into Thane's back.

Nageru heard Thane's breath go out of him.

---

The templar Caleth had wounded stepped back, out of the glaive's reach, and thrust at Korroc from there — and Korroc's shield was there, as it was always there.

Thane moved.

He took one step and put a knife into the templar that was fighting Nageru, low in the ribs, and that man went down and did not move again.

Nageru turned to the other one — the one who had put the sword in his arm — and hit him once.

The man dropped.

There was another beside him. Nageru was already moving, and his body did the rest without asking him, the way water does not ask the hill which way is down. Two more blows. The second man fell across the first.

Korroc's hammer cracked twice more on the man in front of him. The man struck back and found shield.

The last of the men who had been on Korroc changed his mind and came at Nageru instead, and he was quick, and he hit him twice.

Nageru let it go through him. He had learned, long ago, that pain was information. This was the information that there were many of them and they were not stupid.

Another templar came out of a doorway and drove a glaive at Korroc, and this time the stone was not enough. It went in. Blood ran down the gold of Korroc's armor, and the dwarf bared his teeth.

---

And then there was the woman.

She stepped out of the dark at the back of the chamber, and Nageru's eyes went to her at once, because everything about her said *here is the one who matters.* A human woman in fitted leather armor. And on her forehead, tattooed in black, the horned head of Baphomet, so that the god's face sat over her own.

She had a crossbow. She raised it and loosed at him.

Nageru's hand came up and closed on the bolt a hand's breadth from his chest.

He did not think about it. He had done it on a bridge, and in a gallery full of archers, and he had long since stopped being surprised. He turned it in his fingers and threw it back at her the way it had come.

It struck her.

She barely flinched.

Something else came out of a doorway and tried to put a knife in Thane's back, and Thane twisted and it missed. A templar near Nageru put a glaive into him, and it hurt.

And then, near Thane, something else stepped out.

It was not a man.

It had wings — ragged, leathery, folded tight — and the body of a bull, thick-necked and heavy-shouldered and all muscle, and the head of something that was half a bull and half a dragon, horned and scaled and with a mouth full of fangs. It came out beside him, and it lowered those horns, and before Thane could turn it drove them into his back.

Thane went forward three steps.

Caleth moved.

He crossed the space to Thane in a heartbeat, put his hand on the dwarf, and spoke a word — and Nageru, who had watched Caleth do that same thing more times than he could count, waited for Thane to waver and vanish.

Thane did not vanish.

He stood there bleeding with the bull-thing at his back, perfectly visible.

"*Saint's mercy*," Caleth snarled — not the mild oath, the other one — and Nageru saw his face and understood: the spell had gone into Thane properly, and something in this place had caught it and held it and would not let it do its work.

A templar came at Caleth. The first blow landed. The second, Caleth swept aside on Radiance's shaft, and came off the parry with the point, and put it through the man.

Thane was moving. He tumbled clear of the woman and the bull-thing, rolled under a templar's blade, and came up on the other side with a stoppered bottle already in his hand. He pulled the cork with his teeth and drank.

---

Nageru stood very still in the middle of the chamber.

He breathed in, once, all the way down.

His masters had a word for what he did then, and it was not a word that translated. It meant something like *gathering the self into one place.* All the parts of him that were attending to the blood and the pain and the men with blades and the grief that was still coming up through the stone into his feet — he took all of them and drew them in, the way a hand closes, until there was nothing left of him that was not one thing.

The fire came.

It came up his arms and burst out of his fists. And under the fire, below it, he reached down past everything his order had taught him into the other place — the deeper place, the gold place that the wardstone had opened in him, that did not belong to any teacher — and took hold of it.

He took one step, to the woman.

He hit her five times.

He was not aware of choosing where. His fists went where there was an opening, and there was always an opening, and each blow carried the whole of him, and each blow was a prayer and each blow was a law. Chest. Throat. Ribs. Ribs. Face.

Her blood struck the wall behind her.

She went down on the floor.

Behind Nageru, Korroc had laid his own hand flat on his chest, and gold light washed down the dwarf — and then Korroc lifted his hand, and the light went out from him in a wave, over all four of them, and Nageru felt torn places in him knit.

A templar with a sword put it into Korroc as he did it. It should have gone deep. It stopped at the skin, as though there were stone there. Another man's glaive came at him and met the shield.

A templar lunged for Caleth, and Caleth was not there. He was across the room.

A glaive went into Nageru's side.

And the bull-thing came at him, horns and hooves, and battered him — but he was one thing now, gathered and still, and the blows struck him and he gave with them and they did not do as much as they should.

Then the woman was gone.

Nageru saw it. Her body was lying on the floor where he had put it, and then it was not. No flash. No sound. Only a space on the stone where a woman had been, and a smear of blood with nothing at the end of it.

And in the same instant, the bull-thing that had been battering him was gone as well.

Nageru stood there with his fists still burning and nothing in front of them.

---

The rest was only the end of it.

Caleth stepped in and put Radiance into the templar on Korroc. The man who had tried and failed to reach Caleth turned and came at Thane, and struck him once, and Thane took one step toward him and the man went down under the knives and did not get up.

Nageru turned to the nearest templar and hit him once, and felt the skull give, and the man dropped.

He turned to the next, the one on Korroc, and hit him twice, and that one dropped too.

Korroc stepped toward one of the last two and put his hammer into him twice. The man struck back twice and met shield both times. Caleth went at the same man and the man slid aside from the glaive at the last instant.

Thane found the templar who had tried to knife him in the back earlier, and stabbed him twice.

Nageru closed on the last one near him.

The man was quick. He got his blade up and jabbed it into Nageru hard, under the ribs, and it went in.

Nageru took the blade.

He took it and did not stop, and his fists came around anyway, and the man's eyes had just enough time to understand before he dropped.

One templar was left standing, in front of Thane.

Across the room, Korroc lifted his hand, and two rays of fire leapt from his palm and crossed the whole chamber and struck the man in the chest.

The man fell.

And there was silence.

---

It lasted perhaps two heartbeats.

Then the floor began to tremble.

Not a quake. Nageru knew quakes; there were quakes near the Sunken Fist, and they were violent, and they felt like the world trying to throw you off. This was not that. This was deep. This was a pulse. This was like standing on the chest of something very large that was waking up.

The stone under his feet hummed.

And light came.

It came in the cracks. Gold and white, bright as a summer noon, running through every crack in the floor and every fissure in the walls and every chip and gouge in every carving, racing along them like water finding its way downhill, until the whole chamber was laced with threads of it.

The statues shimmered, as if he were seeing them through water.

Nageru heard, very far away, a clatter — and turned his head a fraction, and saw Caleth's legs go out from under him as if the floor had tilted, and the half-elf go down hard on his back on the stone. He saw Korroc, planted, not moving at all, his boots rooted to the floor the way a dwarf's feet root to the earth. He saw Thane drop low into a crouch, knees bent and weight down, riding the shaking floor the way a man rides the deck of a boat.

Then his friends were very far away.

---

The world grew quiet.

The shaking stone, the sound of his companions, all of it seemed to go a long way off, as though he were hearing it from the bottom of a deep clear pool. The light spread. It went across every wall and every pillar.

And then he saw them.

Monks.

Dozens of them.

At first they were only a blur — shapes, silhouettes, streaks of motion flickering from one end of the chamber to the other faster than any eye should be able to follow. But he *could* follow them. He found he could. And they were monks. They were men and women in simple robes, and they moved with a grace that he had spent his whole life reaching for and had only ever touched.

Wherever they passed, the temple mended.

A hand went across a shattered statue, and the stone flowed back together under it. A palm swept over a wall hacked with Abyssal filth, and the filth was gone, and the carving beneath it came back sharp and clean. Pillars that had been cracked stood whole. Floor that had been fouled was bare and scrubbed and pale.

No dust rose under their feet.

No one spoke.

There was only the sound of quiet work.

Nageru stood in the middle of it and understood, with a slow completeness that went all the way down to his feet, that the temple was not being rebuilt.

It was being *remembered.*

---

Light was on him.

He looked down at his own hands, and the gold that always sat faint about him — the glow that was his blood and not his skin — was not faint any longer. It had swelled. It had come up over his hands and his arms and his whole body, until he was wreathed in it, standing in it the way a man stands in a warm river.

It did not burn.

It felt like being known.

---

He looked up at the great statue on its platform.

It was changing too.

The monks had been at it. They moved over it like sparrows over a fallen loaf, and where their hands went, the horns came away like wax. The goat's head smoothed. The leer went out of the face and something else came into it. The carved flesh lost its hide and its hair and its hooves and became a man — a man, seated on the stone with his legs crossed, each foot upon the opposite thigh, his hands resting open on his knees.

As he had been sitting all along.

*They carved a demon over him*, Nageru thought, *and they could not change how he sat.*

He knew the face.

Every monk of his order knew that face.

The monks had finished.

One by one, they were stopping — setting down their hands, stepping back from the work, becoming still, the blur going out of them. And one of them turned.

And, unlike the others, he walked.

---

He was old.

He was older than anyone Nageru had ever seen. Older than Master Borin. Older, Nageru thought, than any mortal should be. His face was lined deep with years and his hair was white and thin.

And there was no weakness in him at all.

Every step he took was exact. There was no wasted motion in him anywhere — none — and Nageru, who had spent his whole life trying to take the waste out of his own body one breath at a time, watched this old man walk across a trembling floor and knew that he was looking at the end of the road he had been walking since he was a child.

His robes were plain and untouched by time.

He stopped in front of Nageru.

He looked at him.

And he bowed.

Not as a master bows to a student, with a small courteous dip of the head. Not as a student to a master. Nageru had given and received both a thousand times and knew them in his bones. This was the other bow. The one given between equals. The one that says *I see you, and what I see, I honor.*

Nageru could not move.

When the old monk spoke, his voice was calm and certain as flowing water, and it went out over the whole chamber, and the trembling of the stone was quiet under it.

"Thank you."

He straightened a little.

"You have returned what was lost."

His smile was warm. Patient. And something else, which Nageru could not at first name, and then could — because he had seen it on his father's face, and his mother's, more times than he could count.

*Proud.*

"We have waited through generations," the old monk said. "For this moment."

He bowed again. More deeply.

"For the day our brother would awaken."

He lifted his head.

His eyes met Nageru's.

His eyes were amber.

---

And Nageru felt a thing he had never felt in his life.

He had been a minor miracle. He had been a beacon. He had been *marked by the gods*, which was what the brothers and sisters said about him when they thought he could not hear, and what the elders said when they knew he could. He had been loved — truly loved, by Elara, by Kaelen, by his masters — and he had never for a single day of his life doubted it.

And he had never, for a single day of his life, *belonged* to anything the way the people around him belonged to things.

He had been the answer to a question nobody would say out loud.

He had carried that silence like a stone in his chest for so long that he had forgotten it was a stone. He had thought it was his chest.

The old monk was looking at him.

*Recognition.*

*Acceptance.*

*Home.*

"Welcome home," the old monk said.

A heartbeat passed.

"Son of Irori."

---

Nageru did not fall down.

Later, he would not understand how.

The old man's smile widened, very slightly. He turned. And he walked away from Nageru, unhurried, deeper into the restored temple — past the platform, toward the statues that stood flanking it — and as he went, he spoke again, without turning, gently, over his shoulder.

"You still have work to do," he said. "We will wait for you."

He passed behind one of the statues.

He did not come out the other side.

There was no door there. There were no footprints on the pale scrubbed floor. There was only the statue, and the wall behind it, and nothing.

---

The light went out of the cracks.

It did not fade. It simply was not there any more, the way a sound is not there after it stops, and the trembling in the floor stopped with it. And the monks were gone — all of them — and the red torches were gone, and in their brackets were plain torches burning a plain yellow flame, and the chamber was only a chamber, very quiet, very clean, with dead men lying on its floor.

The gold went back down over Nageru's skin until it was only the faint thing it had always been.

He breathed.

Sound came back.

He heard someone's ragged breathing, and understood after a moment that it was Caleth's. The half-elf was still on his back on the flagstones where he had fallen. His eyes were open. His mouth was moving. Nothing was coming out of it.

Korroc had not moved at all. He was still standing exactly where he had planted himself, with his hammer hanging in one hand, and he was looking at Nageru, and his face was the face of a man who has just seen the sun come up out of the wrong side of the sky.

Thane was looking at the space behind the statue where the old man had gone. Then, slowly, he turned his head and looked at Nageru.

He did not say a word.

None of them did.

Nageru found that he was walking.

He walked forward across the temple, and behind him he heard Korroc's armor creak at last as the dwarf stirred, and heard Korroc's voice and Thane's, low, and the scrape of a boot and a grunt — the two dwarves getting Caleth up off the floor between them, one on each arm.

He did not look back.

He walked to the foot of the great statue of Irori and knelt.

---

There was a bundle on the stone at the statue's feet.

It had not been there before. He was sure of that. He had looked at that platform through a red haze with a goat's face leering over it, and there had been nothing there but old blood.

It was a folded bundle of cloth.

He picked it up. It was light. He held it a moment in both hands, and then he let it fall open.

Robes.

Simple robes. Undyed, plain, well-made, with the neat close stitching at the collar that he had worn every day of his life. The cut of his order. The look of his order. He knew without holding them up that they would fit him.

His own were torn open at the ribs and stiff with his own blood and a glabrezu's and that of the men on the temple floor.

He held the new robes against his chest.

He bowed his head.

It was not a long prayer. His prayers never were. He did not have the words for this one in any case, and he did not think the one he was praying to needed them.

*Thank you.*

Then he lifted his head, and stood, and turned around.

And the three of them were standing there in the middle of the clean temple floor, Caleth leaning a little on Korroc, all three of them looking at him.

He had no idea what his face was doing.

He did not try to find out.

---

They searched.

It was what came next, and it was something to do, and Nageru was grateful for it with a gratitude so sharp it surprised him. No one living was left in the temple. So they went through it slowly, room by room, as they had gone through a hundred other places, with Thane at the front and Korroc at the rear.

Nobody talked about what had happened.

It sat among them like a fifth companion.

In a side chamber they found a heavy oak table set against the north wall with four round stools around it, and beside it a cabinet — squat, iron-bound, five feet high and five feet wide, with a lock on it that looked as if it had been made to stop a siege.

Thane went to the lock like a man going to meet an old enemy.

He knelt. His picks came out. He worked for a long time, frowning, head tilted, listening to the inside of the thing. Nageru stood back and watched the door.

There was a click.

And mist came out.

It poured out of the gap around the cabinet door, gray and cold, and it did not spread the way mist spreads. It *gathered*. It rose in a column in front of Thane and pulled itself together into a shape, and the shape had four long arms, and before Thane could come up off his knees one of those arms had wrapped around him.

Caleth's glaive came in.

The shape *flowed* around it. Radiance went through the place where the mist had been and the mist was simply somewhere else, curling back around the blade like smoke around a hand.

A second arm wrapped Thane.

And Thane went white. Nageru saw the color go out of his face as if it had been drunk out of him, and his lips went gray, and his eyes lost their focus.

Nageru crossed the room and struck at it.

His fists went into cold. Into wet. It was like punching fog on a winter morning, and he felt the life-cold of it go up his arms to the elbow, and he could not tell whether he had struck anything at all.

Thane twisted.

He was pale and half-drained and there was a thing made of fog wrapped around him twice, and he twisted anyway, and dropped, and slid out from under both arms like a fish out of a hand.

Caleth thrust twice.

This time Radiance found something. Nageru saw the blade go into the gray and *catch*, as a hook catches — and holy light went out along the leaf of the glaive into the mist and lit it from the inside — and the second thrust went in behind the first.

The mist came apart.

It did not scream. It simply thinned, and spread, and was nothing, and the room was cold and then was not.

Thane sat on the floor for a moment with his back against the table leg, breathing, his color coming back slowly.

Then he got up and went back to the cabinet, and finished opening it.

---

There were papers inside.

A great many of them. Thane lifted them out in bundles and set them on the oak table and the four of them stood around it while he sorted, fast, the way he did, and every so often passed a sheet to Caleth, who read quickly and said very little.

It was cult business, most of it. Supplies. Names that meant nothing. But there was more.

"Templars," Thane said. "The Ivory Sanctum again. And Vang." He turned a page. "Xanthir Vang. Orders going out under his name. Or through him."

He turned another.

"And *her*." He tapped the page. "Jerribeth. That elven woman. Lot of references. She's high up. Sends instructions. People answer to her."

Korroc snorted. It was a large snort, and it came all the way up from his boots.

"*Elven*," he said, "is doubtful."

Nobody argued with him.

Nageru thought about a Baphomet symbol that had turned out to be an eye. He thought about a chieftain waiting a year for a beautiful woman who never sent for him. He thought about the kind of creature that could make a man love it and then leave him on a hook, and he did not think Korroc was wrong.

"A succubus," Caleth said quietly. "Probably."

"Probably," Thane agreed.

"We don't know that," Caleth added.

"No," said Thane. "We don't."

He picked up another sheet. "Ivory Sanctum's in the Marchlands. Says so right here." He looked up. "That's helpful."

"Is it?" said Korroc.

"No," said Thane. "It's all the Marchlands. Everything out that door is the Marchlands. It's like telling you the treasure's buried in the ground."

He set it down. He picked up the last page in the bundle, and read it, and then he read it again, and his face went still.

"Well," Thane said.

He laid it on the table and turned it so they could see.

It was a bounty.

Posted under Jerribeth's name. A great deal of money: *one thousand platinum*, the figure written out in full as though whoever had written it had enjoyed writing it. For the capture of a traitor. A succubus. Who had escaped, the page said, from the prisons beneath Drezen.

It did not give her name.

It did not need to.

Nageru saw a cell in his mind. An open door with dead sigils around it. And every wall of that cell, from floor to ceiling, carved with butterflies — hundreds of butterflies, scratched into the granite with fingernails over years — and in the middle of them, cut deeper than all the rest, a prayer.

*I am ready. Finally ready.*

"Arueshalae," Nageru said.

It was the first word he had spoken since the old monk.

The other three looked at him.

"It doesn't say that," said Thane.

"No," Nageru agreed.

"But it's her," Korroc said.

"How many succubi," said Caleth slowly, "do you think have escaped from under Drezen?" He touched the edge of the page. "It's her. And they want her very badly. A thousand platinum is not what you pay for a deserter." He was quiet a moment. "It is what you pay for something you are afraid of."

Thane gathered the papers into a stack, very neatly, squaring the corners against the table, and did not say anything for a while.

Then he said: "She can't teleport."

"Or go ethereal," said Caleth.

"So she's walking." Thane folded the bounty in half. "Somewhere out there. On foot. With that on her head." "Good luck to her."

He said it flatly. He did not seem to notice he had said it. Korroc noticed. Nageru watched the dwarf notice, and watched him decide not to say anything about it.

---

Back in the great chamber, Thane stood in the middle of the floor with his head tilted, looking at the wall under the statue.

"The woman," he said. "She came out from somewhere about here. Right in the middle of this wall. It was like she came straight out of the base of the statue."

He went to it. He put his hands on the stone of the platform and began to work his way along it with his fingers, patient, pressing, the way he had worked along the walls in Drezen. Nageru watched him do it and thought of the treasury, and the chapel, and a seam twenty feet up a blank wall.

It took Thane less than a minute.

There was a soft click, and a section of the platform's front swung inward on a pivot, and a breath of cold stale air came out.

Caleth looked at the empty place on the floor where the woman's body had been.

"She's not dead," he said.

The others looked at him.

"She may be. But I don't think so." He crouched by the smear of blood. "Her body went, and her beast went, at the same instant. No word. No gesture. She was on the floor. She could not have done it herself." He looked up. "A contingency. She had a spell laid on herself ahead of time, waiting — *if I am brought down, take me away.* And it took her. And the animal with her."

"Where?" said Korroc.

"I don't know," Caleth said. "Somewhere she thought was safe."

"Could be she died on the way," Thane said.

"Could be."

"Could be she's lying in a bed somewhere right now with a healer over her," said Korroc, "cursing our names."

"Could be that too."

Nageru looked at the blood on the floor and remembered the five blows, and remembered her blood on the wall, and did not know what he hoped.

---

Behind the door in the platform was a room.

The runes were the first thing. They covered the whole southern wall of it — a wall that bulged strangely outward, as though something on the far side were pressing on the stone — and they were painted, carefully, precisely, line by line, in blood. And the blood was fresh. It had not browned. It shone.

There was a bed against the southwest corner, and a writing desk against the southeast.

And against the north, a pit.

Ten feet across and ten feet deep, its floor thick with bones. Some of them were animal bones. Nageru looked at the others for a long moment, and then did not look at them any more.

"Her pet's bed," he said.

Nobody answered.

The clean light of the restored temple fell in through the doorway behind them and stopped at the threshold of that room, as though it had been told it was not welcome.

Nageru stood in the doorway a while.

Then he stepped back out, into the light.

---

The temple was clear.

And the temple was *clean*. Not scrubbed; he had scrubbed a great many fouled places beside Korroc in the past year and he knew what scrubbed felt like. This was not that. The grief was gone out of the stone. The anger was gone. When he put his bare feet on the floor now, it did not rise up into him. It lay under him like the floor of a room in a house where people are asleep.

He walked the length of it.

He found the carvings the monks had given back, and he stood before each of them in turn.

He found the statue that the old man had walked behind, and he went and stood behind it, where there was only wall. He put his hand on the wall. It was only stone.

*You still have work to do. We will wait for you.*

He did not know how long he stayed.

Long enough that the light through the great opening moved across the floor. Long enough that Korroc sat down on the steps and took his helm off. Long enough that Thane came and stood a few paces off, as Thane sometimes did, not speaking, simply there, and then went away again.

It was Caleth who came in the end.

He came slowly and he stopped at a courteous distance, which was very like him, and he said: "We should go back. Irabeth will want to know. And wherever that woman went, we should not assume she won't send something back."

"Yes," Nageru said.

He looked up at the statue of Irori one more time.

Then he picked up the folded robes from where he had set them on the step, and followed his friends out into the day.

They climbed the path back up the valley wall, and found the wagon and the horses behind their boulder exactly where Korroc had left them, and Korroc made a point of looking at Thane about it.

Thane pretended not to see.

---

Nobody spoke of it on the road home.

Not the first day. Not the second. The three of them were careful around Nageru in a way they had never been careful around him before — not afraid, not *that* — but careful, the way people are careful carrying something full to the brim.

He did not know what to do with it.

He did not know what to do with any of it.

On the second night he sat up past his watch and took Sister Lyra's letter out of his wrappings and read it by the last of the fire.

*Those who established it were waiting for something.*

*Or perhaps someone.*

*Do not allow those words to shape what you expect to find.*

He had not. He was sure of that. He had not let himself even think the word *fane* in all the way out.

It had not helped at all.

He thought of his mother, a scribe, copying the old books by lamplight, weeping at the news of Drezen. He thought of his father, who had mended the waterwheel again although it did not need mending. *A good craftsman is never truly finished.* He thought of the two of them at the gate of the Sunken Fist, the day he walked out of it. He thought of *Son of Irori*, and *our brother*, and of a minor miracle, and of all the things that the elders had said when they thought he could not hear.

He did not know what those words meant for Elara and Kaelen.

He did not know what they meant for him.

He knew that the old man had meant them exactly.

*There is always another step*, Lyra had written.

He folded the letter and put it away.

Across the fire Thane lay on his side with his eyes open, watching the coals. He had carried the axe to bed with him. It muttered in its sleep.

Nageru remembered, very clearly, a night march behind a light that could be seen for six miles, and a dwarf on a wagon seat saying *If you need hands for it, say so.*

He had not said so.

The hands had come anyway.

---

They came back to Drezen a little after noon, the better part of a week after they had left it, and they saw the tower before they saw anything else.

It had been whole when they left.

Now half of it was gone. The top of it had simply been torn away, and what was left stood up against the sky like a broken tooth, and the rubble of it had come down across the wall-walk and into the courtyard below in a long gray slide of stone.

Thane stopped the wagon.

Nobody told him to.

Then he shook the reins and drove on, faster, and nobody told him to do that either.

The gate was open and the gate-guard waved them through without a challenge, and inside there were people running. Soldiers. Paladins with no helms on. Men with stretchers. Men carrying buckets and men carrying stone. It was not panic — Irabeth did not permit panic — but it was the ragged, shouted, barely held-together kind of order that is only one bad hour away from it.

A soldier saw them come in and ran straight at the wagon.

He was young, and he knew them. He grabbed the side of the wagon box and looked up at them.

"Irabeth's in the command center," he said. "She'll want to see you. Right away. I'm sure she will."

---

The command room was full of men coming and going.

Irabeth stood in the middle of it, over the great table, with her hands flat on the wood and her head down. There were no maps on the table. There were things. Scattered over it, laid out the way things are laid out when they have been brought in from a place and nobody yet knows what else to do with them.

A helm, dented on one side.

A sword with a bent blade.

Pieces of armor. A gauntlet. A strap. A buckle.

And a staff.

It was broken. It had snapped a third of the way along its length, clean through, and the two pieces lay side by side on the table with the break between them, and the wood of it was old and pale.

Thane stopped in the doorway.

"Is that —" he said. "Is that Aravashnial's staff?"

Beside Nageru, Caleth went completely still.

Nageru felt it more than saw it. It was not the stillness Caleth wore when he was counting. It was the other kind. The kind that comes over a man when the count has come in and the number is one he cannot say out loud.

Irabeth lifted her head. Her face was gray.

It was Aron Kir who spoke first. He was standing at the far end of the table, and his flat tired voice was flatter than Nageru had ever heard it, and he was frowning.

"The day after you left," Aron said, "the citadel was attacked."

He looked at the staff. His frown went deeper.

"A dragon. Six legs. It flew. And it went for the courtyard. It kept coming back to the courtyard." He put a hand flat on the table beside the broken staff. "We had to get everyone out of the courtyard. We couldn't do it with that thing on top of us."

Nobody spoke.

"Aravashnial went up the tower," Aron said. "With four soldiers. To draw it off. He started summoning creatures — and it worked. It got its attention. It turned away from the courtyard and went for them."

He stopped.

"It went for the tower," Aron said. "And the tower came down. With the five of them on top of it."

Irabeth closed her eyes.

"And as they fell," Aron said, "the dragon caught them. All five. In the air. And it carried them off."

He took his hand off the table.

"Nobody has been able to find them," he said. "Not since."

The command room was full of the sound of men running in the corridors outside.

Inside it, nobody moved.

Nageru looked at Caleth.

The half-elf was looking at the broken staff. He was looking at the place where it had snapped, and his face was doing nothing at all.

And then Caleth made a sound.

It was very small. It was not a word. It was the kind of sound a man makes in his sleep. Nageru would not have heard it at all in a room with any noise in it.

He heard it.
