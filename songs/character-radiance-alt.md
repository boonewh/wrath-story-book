## Why this version exists

`character-radiance.md` reads well and sings long. Its lines run **12–14 syllables**, which is a lyric's length, not a hook's. "Raise Me Higher" — the Chapter 6 track this whole thing is a sequel to — runs **8–11**, in tight rhymed couplets, and that is why it is the one you can still hum.

This cut carries **the identical message** at that shorter measure:

- The weapon speaks and has only light. *(hammer / sword / glaive, "a blade has no mouth," white or dark)*
- It has **already lost this war once, at Drezen's gate**, and then spent fifty years behind glass being kept safe, which was the insult.
- What it wants is **not to be raised and not to be carried — to not be set down again.** Its whole history is being put away: it fell out of Yaniel's hand, sat fifty years in a museum case, and in Ch 6 was wrapped in oilcloth and pushed under Thane's bedding. So the ask is to stay in the war: **keep me in the fight.**
- Caleth read its light wrong once (the false Iomedae) and right once (the cell), and the forge is where it finally consented.
- Yaniel, the banner, the gate, and its name at the close.

**The one structural addition.** The Ch 6 chorus ends on a doubled hook — ***"Raise me higher, raise me higher."*** So this chorus ends on the same shape with the demand changed: ***"Keep me in the fight. Keep me in the fight."*** Two songs, one chorus architecture, seventy years and one whole character between what they ask for — **being lifted** in Ch 6, **not being left behind** in Ch 19. That is the sequel stated in the form itself, not just the words.

**Why "keep me in the fight" and not "spend me" or "use me."** Both of those make Caleth the one expending Radiance, which is wrong for the story — he does not consume the blade, he fights beside it — and both border on innuendo the table would snicker at. *Keep me in the fight* is a partner's want, in soldier's language, and it rhymes with **white**: *"Take that road again. I'll come up white. / Keep me in the fight."* The blade names what it gives and what it wants in one breath. That makes verse 1's *"I come up white. Or I do not"* load-bearing — it plants the rhyme the hook pays off.

**The tag stays identical in all three choruses.** The want has to go unanswered inside the song or it stops being a hook. The affirmation lives in the outro — *"He keeps coming back"* — quiet and near-spoken, after the chorus has stopped asking.

**Title.** *Keep Me in the Fight*, for the same reason Chapter 6's track ended up called *Carry Me* — the doubled line at the bottom of the chorus is what a listener walks away with. *Only the Light* still fits (it is in the final chorus) if you would rather the pair share a name. **Do not leave the title field blank.**

## Style (paste into Suno style box)

Propulsive 1980s neon synth-pop — fast, urgent, cinematic. ONE register throughout, no genre change. Bright saw-lead synth hook cycling as the main melody from the first bar to the last. Relentless four-on-the-floor kick, gated-reverb snare on 2 and 4, driving eighth-note analog bass pulse, glittering arpeggios underneath. Emotional male vocal pushed high and urgent — clipped and punchy in the verses, belted wide open on the chorus, near-spoken in the outro. Short lines delivered as hooks, one phrase to a bar, the repeated last line of each chorus chanted and doubled. Bright major-key surface with real ache under it, NOT an empty party track. Swells WITHIN the one register — verses driving and contained, chorus blooming enormous, bridge stripped to bass pulse and vocal. No tempo changes, no key changes. No acoustic guitar, no strummed strings, no country, no folk, no metal. Synthesizers only. No instrumental padding between sections. End sharp on one bright synth hit, not a fade.

## Troubleshooting

**If "Keep me in the fight" isn't doubled or doesn't land as the hook:** this is the whole point of the cut and worth regenerating for. Tag it `[Chorus - final line doubled, chanted, full lift]`.

**If the white/fight rhyme gets slurred:** *"I'll come up white. Keep me in the fight"* is the hook's hinge — the rhyme is what makes Suno close the line cleanly instead of trailing off. If either word mumbles, regenerate.

**If it doesn't read as a weapon singing:** the identifiers are load-bearing — *hammer / sword / glaive* and *a blade has no mouth* in V1, *a blade has no way to say* in V2, *Yaniel* and *Radiance* at the close. If Suno mumbles any of those, regenerate rather than accept it.

**If "white" comes back sounding like "right":** V1's *"I come up white. Or I do not"* sets the contrast in one couplet. If it still fails, tag `[Verse 1 - enunciate]` before changing the word.

**If verse 4 gets sung smooth:** it must not be. The short lines are the effect. `[Verse 4 - clipped, one line per bar, hard stops]`.

**If acoustic guitar or any strummed string appears:** the recurring Suno failure on this project. Regenerate with `NO acoustic guitar, NO strummed strings, NO country, NO folk — pure electronic, synthesizers only`.

**If the bridge doesn't strip back:** "I said yes" is the emotional center and needs air. `[Bridge - bass pulse and vocal only, no drums]`.

**If the outro is sung big:** it must not be. `[Outro - near spoken, quiet, no drums]`.

## Canon Notes

Same canon as the long cut — see [character-radiance.md](character-radiance.md) for the full sourcing. The short-line compressions worth checking:

- **"I lost this war once at that gate"** — `lore/worldwound.md`: the **Fourth Crusade** was *"a holding action that became a disaster"* and **Yaniel died at Drezen's gates** carrying this sword. `README.md` establishes the crusades as one continuous war — *"ongoing so long that crusaders no longer count campaigns."* A weapon at that gate in the Fourth and at it again in the Fifth is in the same war.
- **"He never blocks. He steps aside… He pays half a step of ground"** — Ch 18, compressed: *"Caleth did not block. He slid… caught the shadow's arm on Radiance's flat and turned it aside, and the whole cost of the exchange to him was a half-step of ground."* Caleth's **Virtuous Bravo** panache and parry (`characters/caleth.md`).
- **Verse 3, the cell** — Ch 17. ⚠ Radiance dimmed at the cell that was **still lit, glowing red**; Caleth *"gave it the width of the room."* **What was behind it is never explained** — hence *the light on it was not mine.* The word **door is deliberately avoided** — doors are Thane's motif.
- **Verse 4** — Ch 18, read closely. The banner falls down the tower and **Radiance does nothing.** Only after the gates go in and the crusade walks through does it come up *"slow — and then it flared… and it did not go all the way down again."* **It did not answer the symbol. It waited for the gate.**
- **The bridge** — Interlude 2. Three failed bindings (*"It's got a latch already"*), then the working seated *"like a weld — pressure, heat, patience, no force,"* and it **accepted him.** *"A smith read the grain"* is Thane, who cut the channels. ⚠ **Aravashnial is not written as old** (`characters/npcs.md`) and is left out of the lyric entirely.
- **"Carry me was my old song"** — the Ch 6 callback, flagged as a former line rather than repudiated, and the doubled chorus tag answers Ch 6's *"Raise me higher, raise me higher"* with *"Keep me in the fight."*
- **"I spent it on you"** — the one surviving use of *spend*, and it is safe: what is spent is **the light**, which is a resource the blade pours out, not the blade itself. The word only causes trouble when Radiance is the thing being spent.

**Not used, deliberately:** what the new working actually *does* (unestablished — needs the GM), what was behind the red cell (never explained), and Caleth's Spireborn / Riftwarden Orphan material (the blade wouldn't know it, and the song is public).
