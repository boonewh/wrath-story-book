🔨 "It Chose Me" — Driving Hard Rock *(alternate — not the session 18 keeper)*

> **This is an alternate, kept on file.** The Session 18 song is [session18.md](session18.md) ("Give Him Back"). This version was built on the Armor of the Pious taking Torag's mark — right about the chapter, wrong about the table, where the armor read as a magic-item footnote and a song about it plays as a riddle. The musical direction here is the one that carried over.

An alternate **Session 18** song for [chapters/18-the-mark-it-chose.md](../chapters/18-the-mark-it-chose.md). Korroc's second per-session song, and the loud one — where [songs/session16.md](session16.md) ("Open It Again") is slow and guilty, this is uptempo riff rock with a gang-shout bridge and a guitar solo.

**One idea, stated plainly enough to land on the first chorus: you cannot force a thing to be what it is.** A man who has spent thirty years making iron do what he says runs into three things in one day that would not take a hammer — a friend he had to disarm without hitting back, a stone he broke on somebody else's word with no proof of his own, and the mark on his chest that nobody put there. Nobody gave it to him. Nobody asked it. It looked at him and decided. That's the whole song, and it's also why he hands a hammer to a man he'd spent four days arguing at instead of arguing at him again.

## Style (paste into Suno style box)

Character count: 812 (spaces included — under the ~1,000 style-box cap, the one real Suno limit)

Driving hard rock, 138 BPM, big open riff, straight four-on-the-floor rock drums with a heavy backbeat. Classic riff-rock guitars — crunchy, mid-gain, rhythm guitar leading the song, a real ripping guitar solo in the middle. Warm loud bass locked to the kick. Male lead vocal: gravel baritone, rough and belted, shouty on the chorus, fully intelligible. Gang-shout backing vocals on the bridge only, several rough male voices chanting together. Anvil and hammer hits used as accents on the downbeat. Mood: defiant, loud, working-man swagger with something bigger underneath it. Energetic and forward-driving start to finish. No choir, no church organ, no orchestra, no ballad section, no slow intro, no acoustic, no tempo change, no key change. Single register, one groove, keep it rocking the whole way through.

## Troubleshooting

1. **If it slows down or turns into a ballad:** The failure this song was rewritten to avoid. Lead the style box with "138 BPM driving hard rock, no ballad, no slow intro, no acoustic passage, never slows."
2. **If the bridge chant comes back as a solo vocal:** It needs several rough voices. Add "gang-shout chorus of rough male voices in unison on the bridge, shouted not sung."
3. **If the guitar solo is skipped:** Make the tag more insistent — `[Guitar solo — long, lead guitar out front, band keeps the riff under it]` — and add "extended guitar solo" to the style box.
4. **If a choir or cathedral pad creeps in:** Same rule as every Korroc song: "solo male vocal only, no choir, no church organ." The faith here is a forge, not a cathedral.
5. **If the vocal comes back clean or polished:** "Gravel baritone, rough, belted, shouted on the chorus" up front. Don't push to a growl — every word of the chorus has to be audible, because the chorus is the entire point.
6. **Keep it distinct from "Open It Again" (session 16).** That one is a 62 BPM doom dirge, guilty and heavy. This one is fast, loud and defiant. If Suno blurs them, push "uptempo, bright crunchy guitars, swagger" here.
