🔨 "The Mark It Chose" — Mid-Tempo Cinematic Forge-Rock

The **Session 18** song for [chapters/18-the-mark-it-chose.md](../chapters/18-the-mark-it-chose.md) — Korroc's second per-session song, and the answer to [songs/session16.md](session16.md) ("Open It Again"). Session 16 was a man finding out what his open hand cost. This one is the same man finding out that the thing he is best at — putting a hammer exactly where a thing wants to come apart — is not how most of what matters gets done.

The through-line a first-time listener needs, with no knowledge of the story: **a smith who has spent thirty years shaping metal by force spends one day discovering the three things he could not shape — a friend he had to disarm without striking back, a stone he broke on somebody else's word and no proof of his own, and a mark that appeared on his chest because something looked at him for four days and decided.** Verse 1 is the disarming: fifty-one years of knowing where that hand goes, using it, and refusing to answer it. The pre-chorus is the faith it took to swing on another man's eyes. The chorus is the swing and the bell-note. Verse 2 is the craft itself — where a thing wants to break, and the borrowed harness quietly taking his measure while he wore it. The bridge is the plain fact of it: **nobody hammered it.** The final chorus is what he does with that the next morning, which is hand a hammer to a man he had spent four days arguing at.

**No names, no plot.** The cousin, the crystal, the banner and the prisoner are shapes only — the camera stays inside Korroc. Paste the LYRICS block into Suno's lyrics box, the STYLE block into the style box, and "The Mark It Chose" into the title field.

## Style (paste into Suno style box)

Character count: 890 (spaces included — under the ~1,000 style-box cap, the one real Suno limit)

Mid-tempo cinematic forge-rock, 92 BPM, steady and unhurried, no acceleration. Opens on a single struck bell allowed to ring out, then a lone heavy drum. Clean ringing guitar figures over deep low strings, building into thick heavy distorted chords at each chorus. Real anvil and hammer-fall hits used as percussion, placed sparingly and let to decay. Male lead vocal only: gravel baritone, low and plain, more stated than sung, awed rather than triumphant, words fully intelligible. No choir, no cathedral organ, no soaring high vocal, no power-metal gallop. Mood: a working man discovering something enormous and very quiet, dawn in a wrecked chapel, reverence without ceremony. Big open space in the mix; let notes ring. Bridge drops to a single low drone under near-spoken vocal. Final section is the heaviest but stays calm and resolved. Single register, one key, one tempo throughout.

## Troubleshooting

1. **If it turns triumphant or power-metal:** The most likely failure. This is not a victory anthem — the banner and the battle are deliberately not in the lyrics. Lead with "no power metal, no gallop, no soaring vocal, calm and reverent, mid-tempo throughout."
2. **If a choir or church organ creeps in:** Same rule as every Korroc song — "solo male vocal only, no choir, no cathedral organ." The reverence here is a forge's, not a chapel's, even though he is kneeling in one.
3. **If the bell and anvil hits get buried or turned into a drum loop:** They are punctuation, not a groove. Add "bell and anvil struck rarely, allowed to ring out fully, not looped, not a rhythm bed."
4. **If the bridge gets sung instead of spoken:** Add (spoken) inline after each bridge line. "Nobody hammered it" is the thesis of the song and has to land flat and plain, twice.
5. **If it sounds like the session 16 song:** It shouldn't — that one is 62 BPM doom, guitar-weight forward, guilty. This one is 92 BPM, open, ringing, and resolved. If Suno blurs them, push "clean ringing guitar, wide open mix, let notes decay" on this one.
6. **The closing line** — "So here. Take the hammer." — should land almost conversational, the way you hand a tool to somebody in a shop. If Suno makes a climax of it, tag it (spoken).
