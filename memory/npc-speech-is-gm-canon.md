When GM material (an interlude, a session note) seems to clash with canon, sort it this way before flagging:

1. **An NPC saying something is the GM saying it.** Aravashnial naming the creature a *neh-thalggu* is the GM's ruling, even where the published bestiary disagrees. The GM wins over published lore (this is an exception to [[ap-spellings-win]], which is about spelling, not about what a thing is).
2. **"Unnamed and never found" describes what the characters knew in that story**, not a ban. An NPC can name it later.
3. **A reveal that closes an open thread is not a conflict.** The open-thread lists exist to stop *me* inventing answers, not to stop the GM giving them. Record it as closed.
4. **Lore and memory files are behind the scenes and follow the story.** If a lore line ("he recognized Caleth from the day they met") conflicts with new material but the chapter prose never stated it, just change the lore. **Only a contradiction with published chapter prose is worth a flag.**

**Why:** In the Interlude 3 pass I flagged all three of these as conflicts. Will's answer (2026-11-21): the neh-thalggu is "from the GM. The GM can do what the GM wants to do… Change lore." The three-moon book: "I don't understand how that is a conflict. It is a reveal." Ch 1: "Lore is behind the scenes. Changing lore is small unless it changes the story."

**How to apply:** Before flagging, ask whether the clash is with chapter prose. If not, update the files and mention it in one line. Do flag real prose contradictions and anything the step 7 questions cover (gaps I would otherwise have to invent).
