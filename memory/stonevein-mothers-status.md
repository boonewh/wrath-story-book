Dagna's Ch 19 letters settle two questions the character files had been holding open — **and both files were wrong, in opposite directions.** Fix them wherever they resurface.

## Helja Stonevein — DEAD

`characters/thane.md` said **"Still living (presumed)"** and listed *"whether his mother is still safe in Kenabres"* as an open thread, twice. **Both are now wrong.**

Dagna gives the order of the losses plainly: they fled Drezen; years became decades; *"your father and Korroc's father disappeared"*; **"and eventually your mother was gone too."** After that there were only three of them — Thane, Korroc, and Dagna. Her closing line to Thane is the chapter's hardest: ***"Your mother should be the one writing this letter."***

**⚠ WHEN AND HOW SHE DIED IS NOT ESTABLISHED. DO NOT INVENT IT.** Dagna knows and did not put it in a letter. It happened **after** the fathers disappeared, and Thane knew his mother well into his adult life — she taught him the lockpicking mantra that is also Alseta's liturgy, and gave him his first dagger.

**The prose consequence that matters:** *the blade Thane wipes after every kill is a dead woman's knife, and has been for some time.* He wiped it three times in Ch 19 and twice in one day at the end. Nobody watched.

**She loved Drezen** — Dagna is explicit that Helja loved it in a way Dagna never did, could stand in its mud and see everything good about the place, and always believed they would go back. **Thane spent three weeks in that fortress thinking only about his father and never once about his mother being there. She was the one who loved it.**

## Dagna Stonevein — ALIVE, in Nerosyan

`characters/korroc.md` and `characters/thane.md` both said **"do not mark her alive or dead."** That is retired: she wrote to both cousins, separately, and said so (*"before you decide you can simply read his and save yourself the trouble, you cannot"*).

She is **the last of the four**, and she intends to come south: *"After seventy-five years, I think it is time I went home."* **Do not bring her to Drezen without the table.**

## Drezen was their home

New lore, and it re-frames nineteen chapters. **Dagna and Helja knew Drezen's streets before either man did**; Thorek and Borin came later; **the four met there and became a family there.** Dagna: *"if there had never been a Drezen, there might never have been either of you."* When it fell seventy-five years ago they took what they could carry, and she long ago stopped calling it an evacuation, *"because you evacuate when you expect to return."*

This is coherent with the existing timeline, not in conflict with it: the fathers helped build the citadel before the fall; the reconstructed blueprints letter was written ~70 years ago, five years after, from memory, by twelve people who had known the place; the ambush came decades later. See [[thane-father-timeline]].

## How to apply

- **Dagna's request is a live, partially-paid errand.** She asked Korroc to walk out into Drezen, find old stone away from the repairs, put his hand on it and tell the city she remembers her — and asked Thane to be there for it, adding *"try not to make fun of him for talking to a wall."* **They did it in Ch 19**, four streets short of the Erastil shrine. Korroc said it out loud; Thane put his own palm on the stone and said nothing.
- **Thane means to write to her about the fathers and has not yet.** *"It had better not be Aravashnial with the ledgers, and it had better not be Korroc on his own."* See [[the-fathers-survived]].
- Dagna's voice is dry, direct, anticipates arguments and closes them in advance, and is warmer than either of her boys would admit. She writes in one sitting in a small upright hand, bearing down on the downstrokes.
