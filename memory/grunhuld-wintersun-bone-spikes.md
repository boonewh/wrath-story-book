**Two facts from Ch 20, and the first one is an error I actually made in drafting and had to be corrected on.**

## ⚠ THE BONE SPIKES ARE WORN, NOT GROWN

The Grunhuld-Wintersun are described everywhere — by Kamilo Dann, by the Ford, and therefore by the crusade — as having **"bone spikes coming through the skin."** That is wrong, and the wrongness is the point.

- **Only Marhevok Grunhuld-Wintersun grew them, and only when he raged.** The transformation is his alone: swelling, arterial skin, horns splitting out of the brow, and bone tearing up out of the flesh along forearms, shoulders and spine.
- **Every other member of the clan lashes old bone and horn to their armor in imitation of him** — drilled at the base, pegged through boiled leather, whipped down with sinew, the bone old and worn smooth where a nervous hand goes.
- Caleth established it off a corpse: **the spike turned in his fingers**, and there was ordinary scarred shoulder underneath.
- **Jestak's horns were *bolted* to her pauldrons — the same practice.** *(She was Grunhuld-Wintersun. Retro-established Ch 20.)*
- **Caleth's reading: camouflage, not worship.** Nobody in the challenge ring was cheering when their liege transformed; they watched it like weather coming over a hill. *"They dress as the thing that eats them so it will look past them."*

**Why it matters beyond the detail:** Jestak read as **evil** to two paladins in one doorway and every word she said was true. Ch 20 put a whole village of people in front of the party who read the same way and were victims. **Both things stay true at once and the prose never lets either cancel the other.**

## Korroc is clanliege

He killed Marhevok in a broken challenge circle and **the tribe passed to him by their own law** — he did not seek it, did not fight the duel, and did not refuse it. About four dozen people, **resettled at Vilareth Ford**, where they doubled the camp. **Beverach** leads in his absence. ⚠ **Korroc promised them that when the war ends they go wherever they want.** Hold him to it.
