**Written 2026-09-20, after the Chapter 22 pass.** Will caught seven separate problems in that draft, one at a time, after fifteen thousand words already existed. **Six of the seven were preventable.** This file exists so the next pass catches them before the prose, not after.

**Why:** the cost is not the correction. Will is generous about corrections and the fixes were usually quick. The cost is that a wrong fact gets *built into* prose — the purple-light error in Ch 22 had a whole paragraph of Caleth's interiority resting on it, and correcting it meant rewriting the passage three times in three different directions. **Catch it in the notes and it costs a sentence. Catch it in the draft and it costs a scene.**

---

## The taxonomy, with the real Ch 22 examples

### 1. ⚠⚠ Filling an ambiguous session note with plausible invention — THE BIG ONE (3 of 7)

The session notes are a GM's shorthand, written fast, for people who were in the room. **They routinely leave out the thing you most need.** In every case below the note read as a *fact* while drafting and only became a *question* once Will pushed back.

- **A colored light is not self-identifying.** The notes said *"a faint pulse of purple light."* The draft read it as a **Nahyndrian crystal** (Ch 18's fakes, Chorussina's great crystal — both established purple). It was **an Abyssal rift**, and the right comparison was the Ch 11 rift at the Ahari that took Aravashnial three castings to close. Getting this wrong cost the chapter its best cold moment and had to be rebuilt from scratch.
- **"He cast a spell that teleported the four of them" does not say who travels.** The draft had Caleth go with the rescued soldiers and fold back. He **only sent them** and stayed in the cave — the harder, more expensive casting, and a character beat.
- **"Started the scrying process again" does not say what the target is.** The draft read it as another attempt at Aravashnial. It was **the templar who took him** — and it failed precisely *because* Caleth had never seen the man's face, which is a far better scene.

**These three have nothing in common except that the note was silent and the draft was confident.**

### 2. Running over canon that was already written down (2 of 7)

- **Aravashnial is NOT old.** `characters/npcs.md` says, in bold: *"long-lived but **not elderly**… do NOT write him as an 'old man.'"* The draft called him an old man or old elf **fourteen times**, and invented *"eighty-year-old"* three times on top of it. Elves find it insulting; a couple of centuries is young. He can still remember the land west of Drezen being green — that costs nothing.
- **Thane's god is Alseta, not Torag.** Torag is Korroc's. The cousins got crossed. `characters/thane.md` has a whole Faith section.

**Both were already in the files. Reading is not the same as checking.**

### 3. Inventing texture that reads as canon (2 of 7)

- ***"Ramson's Ford"*** — a place name invented for one clause of a secondhand witness account. Replaced with an unnamed river crossing, which is what a copied account would actually give you.
- ***"eighty-year-old"*** — a number invented to get pathos, contradicting established canon.

**Invented proper nouns are the worst kind of drift, because they look like canon on reread.** A detail that is *vague* is safe. A detail that is *specific and wrong* gets quoted back later.

### 4. Plausibility and finality (2 of 7)

- **Eleven minutes to buff before a fight.** That is a round or two of game time. Now three minutes. **Sanity-check any in-scene duration against what the table would actually have done.**
- ***"A dead man's office."*** Aravashnial is taken, not dead, and the whole chapter is about going to get him. **Narration must not carry a finality the POV character has no grounds for** — especially about a character on an open thread. A second instance (*"a dead man's name"*) was ambiguous enough to read as Aravashnial when it meant Vang.

### 5. Genuinely missing from canon (1 of 7 — the only unpreventable one)

- **Caleth speaks Dwarven**, and has for several sessions. It was nowhere in the files. Now recorded in `characters/caleth.md` with the full confirmed language roster.

## ✅ THE FIX: two steps that did not exist before

### STEP A — Surface the interpretive calls BEFORE writing. *(This is now step 6.5 in CLAUDE.md.)*

After reading the session notes and the canon, and **before drafting a word**, go back through the notes and list every place where **the prose will have to commit to something the notes do not state.** Put them to Will in one batch.

Ask about, at minimum:
- **Anything identified by appearance rather than name** — a colored light, a shape, a sound, a creature described but not named.
- **Who moved, and who stayed.** Teleports, retreats, and "the group went" are chronically ambiguous.
- **What a repeated action was aimed at** — a second casting, another attempt, "tried again."
- **Any number the prose will have to state** — distances, durations, counts, how long something took.
- **Any proper noun the prose wants and the notes lack** — a place, a rank, a title. **Do not invent one. Ask, or write around it.**
- **Anyone's status** — alive / dead / missing / unknown, and *who in the party believes which*.

**This works.** Will answered eight such questions for Ch 22 in a single message, and every answer improved the chapter — *"the GM said there is family there,"* *"the party saw the vision,"* *"he only sent them."* The questions were right; they were just asked after fifteen thousand words instead of before one.

⚠ **A batch of questions before drafting is cheap and welcome. A batch of corrections after drafting is expensive.** Do not skip this to seem efficient.

### STEP B — Grep the finished draft before handing it over. *(Now in CLAUDE.md's checklist.)*

Already standing: American English, `(two|three|several) years`, and name spellings. **Add these three, which Ch 22 needed and did not have:**

1. **Character-trait violations.** For every named character with real page time, open their file and grep the draft against their hard rules. For this cast: `old (man|elf)|elderly` for Aravashnial; `Torag` appearing near Thane; `golden` near Nageru's skin; `grey`.
2. **Invented proper nouns.** Grep the draft for capitalized place and person names and confirm each against `characters/`, `lore/` or the session notes. Anything unmatched is either invented or needs asking about.
3. **Finality language about open-thread characters.** `dead|died|corpse|body|grave|mourn|grief` near anyone who is missing rather than confirmed dead. Right now that means **Aravashnial**, **the four Stonevein parents** (two alive, one dead, one unestablished), and **Jerribeth**.

## Addendum — Ch 23 (2026-09-27)

**Step A worked.** Twenty-nine questions went out before a word was drafted; Will answered them in one message and **every answer changed the prose** — Korroc must *not* see the Spiral, Thane (not Korroc) tells Dagna, Dagna watched from the walls, Caleth was trying to grab Thane *and fold them both out*. Post-draft corrections dropped from seven to two:

- **A missing question in the notes.** The notes showed four questions to the dead man; Will's answer said *"should be five."* The draft **manufactured a fifth by splitting the first question in two** instead of asking what the fifth was. It was ***"Do you know if they got out?" — "No."*** — the most important answer in the scene. ⚠ **When the author says a count is wrong, ask what is missing. Do not pad to the number.**
- **An anachronistic image.** *"A clock on a wall"* in a Mendevian frontier fortress. Now an hourglass. **Grep metaphors for modern furniture** — clocks, watches, minutes-as-counted-units, trains, glass windows where there would be shutters.

## What NOT to take from this

**Do not get timid.** The Ch 22 draft was accepted almost entirely — Will's corrections were surgical, and the prose, structure, POV call and the big swings (the ceiling as *"knowing wasn't enough"* made physical; ending on the name) all landed without a note. **The problem was never ambition. It was confidence in places where the notes were silent.**
