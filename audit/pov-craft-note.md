## 2. Voice: "establish it early, then trust it"

The style guide's test is that a signature trait shouldn't show up in more than one paragraph in three or four. Counting the most repeated tic phrases per chapter gives a rough picture:

| Chapter (POV) | Words | Tic | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 (Nageru) | 10.1k | "the breath went down / came up" | **9** |
| 9 (Nageru) | 16.0k | same | **15** |
| 13 (Nageru) | 11.4k | same | 1 |
| 21 (Nageru) | 12.2k | same | 0 |
| 7 (Thane) | 15.0k | "filed / files / filing" | **10** |
| 11 (Thane) | 12.6k | same | **9** |
| 19 (Thane) | 20.8k | same | 1 |
| 6 (Caleth) | 12.3k | "arithmetic" | 8 |
| 15 (Caleth) | 9.5k | same | **10** |
| 22 (Caleth) | 15.9k | same | 5 |
| 16 (Korroc) | 10.5k | stone-reaching ("reached down", "into the stone") | **10** |

**The early Nageru chapters are exactly the case the style guide describes:** the breath refrain sets the pace of the prose instead of flavoring it. **Ch 13 and Ch 21 show the fix working.** Nageru's stillness shows in how he enters a fight, and the breath motif comes back only at the hinges. The same arc is visible in Thane, whose "filing" tic drops from 10 to 1 between Ch 7 and Ch 19. Caleth's "arithmetic" is the trait that hasn't come down yet; Ch 15 and Ch 22 lean on it hardest.

## 3. Chapters that model the method

- **Ch 13 (Nageru).** Voice set in the first page, then the scenes run. The stillness breaking ("*NO!*") lands because it has been rationed.
- **Ch 19 (Thane).** The four-source reveal, Aravashnial arguing against his own case, "a nuisance in a ledger", "I could not have done that", and the wrong-distance motif (bow, pit, scale) resolved by the punch dagger. It's the best-built chapter in the book.
- **Ch 18 (Korroc).** A mechanical item, the Armor of the Pious, becomes the character's thesis ("I can't hammer a man into a shape"), backed by a remembered line from his apprentice years: "Which is a nice thought or a terrible one, depending how honest you are."
- **Ch 21 (Nageru).** "They carved a demon over him, and they could not change how he sat." It's planted in the same chapter at the first sight of the statue's posture. This is the cleanest image-plant in the book.
- **Ch 15 → Ch 20 → Ch 23 (Caleth).** Ch 15 opens "Knowing wasn't enough." Ch 20 inverts Terendelev's charge. Ch 23 moves the origin of his future-running from the Lantern Seer to his father ("children are not equations"). That's a three-chapter arc for one character.

## 4. Length

Mean chapter length was about 11.5k words for Chs 1–12 and about 15k for Chs 13–23 (Chs 19 and 23 are both close to 20k). This isn't a problem; the long chapters are the best ones. But if the book reaches 60 chapters at the recent rate, it will run to roughly 850k words.

## 5. Two craft risks worth watching

- **Meta slips at climaxes.** Both fourth-wall slips ("three chapters", "seventeen chapters") fall at moments of high interiority. A pre-handoff grep for `\bchapters?\b` inside prose would catch them.
- **The "four days" reflex.** Ch 18 and Ch 18.5 both reach for "four days ago" when they mean yesterday, and Ch 22 reaches for "four months" when it means about six weeks. The drafting checklist only guards "two years." One more check would help: **any stated elapsed time, compared with the last chapter that fixed one.**
