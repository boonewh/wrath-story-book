# The Worldwound

The bleeding rift in the world. The campaign's primary antagonist as a place. The wound the Mendevian Crusade has been bleeding to staunch for nearly a century.

---

## Origin

When **Aroden died** roughly a century ago, the planar barriers of the world weakened. In the northwestern region of what was once the kingdom of **Sarkoris**, the Abyss itself tore through reality, creating a vast gateway through which demons could pour into the mortal world.

This is the Worldwound.

It has not closed. It cannot be closed by ordinary means. It has bled demons into Golarion ever since.

---

## Geography

**Location:** Northwestern Mendev / Eastern former Sarkoris. A vast region of demon-haunted ruin, several hundred miles across in either direction.

**Terrain:** What was once verdant kingdom is now twisted by Abyssal influence — forests of bleeding trees, rivers running with foul liquids, sky perpetually overcast with churning storms, ruined Sarkorian settlements occupied by demonic warlords.

**Borders:** Held — barely — by the **wardstone network** along the Mendevian frontier. Kenabres was one of the major eastern anchor cities.

## The Demonic Forces

### Major Demon Lords with Campaign Relevance

**Deskari — Lord of the Locust Host**
- Senior demonic power behind the wardstone destruction (per the S. V. letter)
- The Worldwound's most active demon prince
- Long-game architect of the campaign's central conspiracy
- Likely a late-campaign antagonist at full mythic scale

**Baphomet — Prince of Minotaurs, Lord of the Ivory Labyrinth**
- The Templars of the Ivory Labyrinth serve him directly
- Cultists wear his bull-headed brand on their bare chests beneath their tabards
- Master of mazes, manipulation, and corruption
- Mid-to-late campaign antagonist
- Holds the Ivory Labyrinth — a vast planar maze in the Abyss
- The party may eventually have to enter the Labyrinth itself

**Khorramzadeh — The Storm King**
- A Balor Lord — among the most powerful demons short of demon princes
- Personally led the attack on Kenabres at Armasse
- Killed Terendelev with a single sword-stroke
- Wields a flaming sword and a flaming whip
- Three times the height of a man, skin of fire and lightning
- Late-campaign personal antagonist; the party will likely face him at mythic level

### Lesser Demons Encountered So Far

**Dretches** (encountered in the maze, Session 3)
- The lowest-rank demons in the Abyssal hierarchy
- Quivering pillar-bodies of curdled flesh on knotted muscle
- Claws, bite, and a fear-howl aura
- Heal rapidly from normal weapon damage; require holy or magical attacks
- Used as expendable summons by Templar cultists

**Derakni** — the locust demon (Session 19, Eagle Rock)
- Horse-sized and airborne on four insect wings. A humanoid upper torso above enormous folded grasshopper haunches, long arms, and a scorpion's tail curled forward over the whole body.
- **Immune to electricity.** Nageru's javelin of lightning did not make it flinch. Cold iron works.
- Summons **swarms of wasp-like creatures** — four at once, placed in the middle of a formation rather than at its edge. The swarms are the real threat to anyone without an area attack; a single fireball cleared all four.
- Fights by **flyby** — sweeping across the ground, striking in passing, and climbing out of reach. Punishes parties that cannot reach altitude.
- **The one at Eagle Rock was banished, not killed.** It is intact on its home plane and has seen the party's faces.

**Shachath** (Session 19, Delamere's tomb)
- **An angel and a demon fused, badly and on purpose.** The front is a flat fanged inhuman face fixed in a permanent contempt that is not about anything in particular — simply the shape the face was left in. **Bulging through the back of the skull is a second face, beautiful, wearing an expression of complete horror.** It screams with both at once when it dies.
- **A possessing spirit.** It rode the cleric **Jesker Helton** for roughly a month, progressively — surfacing as a failed consecration, damaged holy symbols, wrongness that witnesses could feel and not quote, alternating black moods and brightness, and finally a self-desecrated shrine and a determined walk toward suicide. **It was flushed out by a hold person cast on the host, not detected directly.**
- **The victim goes on radiating chaotic evil after the demon is destroyed.** This is residue, not consent. A paladin can tell the difference and should say so out loud.
- Toolkit: flight; **chaos hammer** (damages, and threatens lawful targets with a slow effect); invisibility; short-range teleportation, used to escape being boxed in and to reappear behind the most isolated enemy; claws and a longsword; a tail-strike.

### Demons Likely to Be Encountered Later

## The Mendevian Crusades

### The First Crusade
- Launched within years of the Worldwound's opening
- Pushed the demons back from spreading further east
- Established the original wardstone network
- Many First Crusade dead became the touched — those who took Abyssal taint and fled below the surface, becoming the ancestors of the mongrelfolk (see kenabres.md and factions.md)

### The Second Crusade
- Stabilized the borders
- Established the major crusader cities
- Founded military orders

### The Third Crusade
- A push to retake **Drezen** — a major fortress city deep in the Worldwound territory
- Initially successful, then catastrophically defeated
- Drezen has been in demonic hands ever since

### The Fourth Crusade
- A holding action that became a disaster
- **Thorek Stonevein died here**, as did the paladin **Yaniel** at Drezen's gates
- Severe casualties throughout the Mendevian forces
- Morale never fully recovered

### The Fifth Crusade
- Ongoing at campaign start
- Has been ongoing so long that crusaders no longer count campaigns the way they once did
- More a permanent military state than a discrete operation
- The campaign opens with this crusade in deep trouble — and the wardstone destruction makes everything worse

## Khar-Zadûn — The Lost Sky City

**Critical for Thane's arc.**

**What it was:**
- The legendary Dwarven Sky City
- Torn from the heavens during the first demonic invasions (predating even Aroden's death — older than the Worldwound itself, in some lore)
- Cloud-piercing towers
- Star-forges
- Halls with wind-channels that sang runic music
- Defended by the elite Skyguard (Thorek's order)

**What it is now:**
- Fallen, sealed, or unreachable
- Possibly in the Worldwound itself
- Possibly in a planar pocket
- Possibly under the Worldwound
- *Status: GM to determine*

**Plot relevance:**
- Thane's father dreamed of reclaiming it
- The Skyguard insignia is the family relic
- The line in Thane's song — *"I'll lay your sky-steel insignia / on the chain when the chain is free"* — points to a climactic moment involving Khar-Zadûn
- If Thane stands in his father's lost city, that should be one of the defining moments of the campaign

## The Wardstones

**Function:** Magical anchors at fortified locations along the Mendevian border. Each suppresses the local demonic incursion within its region.

**Status of the Network:**
- **Kenabres wardstone: DESTROYED** (Armasse, Session 1)
- Other wardstones: presumably still functional, but **weakened** by the network damage
- The Templars' coordination implies **other wardstones may be targeted soon**

**Strategic Importance:**
With Kenabres's wardstone gone, the eastern frontier is wide open. The party's mission to deliver intelligence and rally a defense is directly tied to whether Mendev can hold the line at all.

## Places West and South of Drezen *(established Ch 19)*

### The route
Aravashnial's standing advice, and the reason the party does not cut straight south from Drezen: **the direct line runs through genuinely untamed country — the actual border of the Wound, unheld on both sides, and full of swarms.** The safe road is **back to Vilareth Ford** (Caleth can teleport the party there, but not horses or wagons — resupply at the Queen's staging camp in that area) **and then west past Eagle Rock**, which is the only place along that escarpment where a wagon can get up the cliffs. From the top you can travel inland to the Weeping Hills without going around.

⚠ **Direction is canon and has been ruled on: the party travels WEST from Vilareth Ford.** *(A player after-action report said "east." The GM's notes say west twice and are correct.)*

### Eagle Rock
An enormous formation on the lip of the escarpment west of Vilareth Ford: **five ruined towers standing at uneven heights** like broken fingers, and below them the cliff face curving round into the profile of a bird of prey — the hooked beak, the hollow of the eye. At its base, **the remnants of a small ruined fort**, pre-Wound work, thoroughly picked over.

**Demons are known to use it.** Kamilo Dann warned the party that patrols move through the country and that there were reports of demons taking up residence in the Rock itself. **A derakni ambushed them from the top of the formation** — and Thane had scouted the whole approach for the better part of an hour and found nothing, *because a thing that flies leaves nothing to read.* **The derakni was banished, not killed. It is alive on its home plane, intact, with the party's faces in its head.**

⚠ **Eagle Rock is NOT the same place as Delamere's tomb.** The Rock is the way up the cliffs; the tomb is further inland in the Weeping Hills, reached late the same afternoon. *(An after-action report compressed the two. It is wrong.)*

### The Weeping Hills
Geothermal country south of Drezen — vents, hot springs, the ground weeping fire. **Delamere's tomb is at the bottom of a chasm two hundred feet deep**, running away southwest for as far as the eye follows it, with **cascades of lava seeping from Abyssal rifts in the chasm floor and gathering into a river of molten rock.** There is no path down and nowhere obvious to stand at the bottom; the party descended by climbing, rope and pitons, and two silver scales.

**The tomb itself:** a tunnel forty feet in from the chasm floor, opening into a large cavern with the lava river crossing it and **a dozen gaping pits** dropping away on either side. Roof thirty feet, cracked end to end and breathing gas. **The ambient temperature is around 135°F — severe heat; endure elements is not optional.** A small ledge near the center holds **Delamere's smoky crystal coffin**, with a fifty-foot pit between it and the entrance. **The heat stops near the crystal and nobody knows why.**

*(See `lore/items.md` — Delamere's grave goods were deliberately left in place. See `characters/npcs.md` for Delamere and Jesker Helton.)*

### ⚠ The lost fane of Irori — UNLOCATED
Somewhere **in the lands near Drezen** there was once a small, isolated fane dedicated to **Irori**, kept by brothers and sisters devoted to contemplation, discipline and the pursuit of perfection. **When Drezen fell seventy-five years ago all contact ceased; no member of the order returned and no reliable account of its fate ever reached the Sunken Fist.**

**Sister Lyra has charged Nageru with finding it (Ch 19).** The oldest surviving records contain a passage that appears in no later account, suggesting the fane was founded not merely as a place of worship — **that those who established it were waiting for something, or someone.** The surviving text is unclear.

**Do not locate it, populate it, or explain it.** Full detail in `characters/nageru.md`.
